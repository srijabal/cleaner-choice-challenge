Run 2 changed the initial view: purchase history did not improve held-out-household predictions. Log loss rose from 1.5909 without history to 1.5952 with history, and buy/no-buy predictions also worsened. The simpler learned model was retained for exploration, but the original starter performed better on overall log loss. Run 3 confirmed that limitation on the check households without further tuning.

I do not know the best launch combination yet. Run 4 mostly favors $3.49/environment/online for purchase probability, but the evidence is fictional, associations are not causal effects, one fold prefers cleaning, and margins are unknown.

The next test would be one randomized online study comparing $3.49 versus $4.49 and cleaning versus a substantiated environmental message. Keep the product and competing offers fixed, include no purchase, measure actual buying, and follow repeat purchases. Online is a practical test venue, not a proven channel winner. Obtain costs and predefine a worthwhile profit difference and sample size first. Choose $4.49 if the discount does not pay for itself; change the message if cleaning performs better. If uncertainty remains large, do not declare a winner.
